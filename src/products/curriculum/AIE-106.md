# AIE-106 — Speech and Multimodal Systems

**4 credits · 62 hours · 8 modules**

> Voice is the domain where the target employers live, and it is unusually unforgiving: a text product that takes two seconds is fine, and a voice product that takes two seconds is broken. Human conversation runs on a 300–500 ms response window, and most deployed voice agents sit at 1.4 seconds or worse. This course is about closing that gap and knowing exactly which hop to blame.

Domain depth for voice AI specifically, where existing shipped work already provides a foundation and the target employers live.

**By the end you can**

- Build a full ASR → reasoning → TTS loop and account for every millisecond in the budget
- Explain why transport choice dominates perceived latency in voice agents
- Measure ASR quality properly, including on accented and code-switched speech
- Handle barge-in, turn-taking and partial results
- Decide whether a document problem needs vision at all, and show the measurement behind the answer

**Sitting rule.** Every item is completable in one sitting. Nothing exceeds 180 minutes, and anything over 90 declares its own checkpoints.

> **Advanced standing.** Advanced standing awarded 1 of 3 credits. Read Any Language and 1 Percent More Fluent already evidence synthesis integration at level 2 — live voice and model enumeration, quota handling, shipped. LearnIndo's speech-to-text path evidences ASR at level 1, which is 'touched it' and does not exempt anything. Nothing carries over for streaming, latency budgeting, quality measurement or turn-taking, which is most of the course.

### M2 — Measuring recognition properly

*420 minutes*

- **M2.1 · Common Voice: a massively multilingual speech corpus** — reading, 45 min, tier 0  
[Common Voice: A Massively-Multilingual Speech Corpus](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06670)
- **M2.2 · WhisperX: time-accurate long-form transcription** — reading, 45 min, tier 0  
[WhisperX: Time-Accurate Speech Transcription of Long-Form Audio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00747)
- **M2.3 · Hugging Face Audio Course: evaluation** — reading, 60 min, tier 0  
[Evaluation metrics for ASR](https://huggingface.co/learn/audio-course/chapter5/evaluation)
- **M2.4 · Word error rate is not enough** — assignment, 150 min, tier 1
  Build an ASR eval harness that reports WER, and then at least two measures that WER hides: entity error rate on names, numbers and codes, and error rate on the words that change meaning. Show a case where WER improves and your application gets worse. That case is the reason this assignment exists. Build it inside the eval suite from AIE-102 M6.4 rather than beside it — what is new here is the metrics, not the plumbing.
  *Checkpoints:* WER harness running → two additional measures implemented → a WER-improves-but-worse case demonstrated
- **M2.5 · The errors that matter** — assignment, 105 min, tier 2
  For one product, classify transcription errors by downstream consequence rather than by linguistic type: harmless, recoverable, and silently wrong. Report the distribution. The third category is the one that determines whether you can ship, and it is almost never measured.
  *Checkpoints:* consequence taxonomy defined → errors classified → distribution reported
- **M2.6 · Retention: recognition metrics and their blind spots** — retention, 15 min, tier 1

### M3 — Accented and code-switched speech

*450 minutes*

- **M3.1 · On the End-to-End Solution to Mandarin-English Code-switching Speech Recognition** — reading, 60 min, tier 0  
[On the End-to-End Solution to Mandarin-English Code-switching Speech Recognition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00241)
  Built on SEAME — spontaneous Mandarin-English code-switching from Singaporean and Malaysian speakers. Your own linguistic environment, treated as a research problem.
- **M3.2 · CS-Dialogue: 104 hours of spontaneous code-switching dialogue** — reading, 45 min, tier 0  
[CS-Dialogue: A 104-Hour Dataset of Spontaneous Mandarin-English Code-Switching Dialogues for Speech Recognition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18913)
- **M3.3 · SeamlessM4T** — reading, 45 min, tier 0  
[SeamlessM4T: Massively Multilingual & Multimodal Machine Translation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11596)
- **M3.4 · Build the eval set nobody has** — assignment, 180 min, tier 1
  Construct an evaluation set of at least 60 utterances across three conditions: Singaporean-accented English, spontaneous Mandarin-English code-switching, and a monolingual control. Include the things that actually break systems here — particles, place names, mixed-language numerals, and terms that switch language mid-noun-phrase. Your own voice alone is sufficient; if you record anyone else, consent must be documented. Reference transcripts must state their own conventions, because how you transcribe a code-switch is a decision, not a fact.
  *Checkpoints:* 60+ utterances across three conditions → transcription conventions documented → consent documented or single-speaker declared
- **M3.5 · Benchmark the vendors on it** — assignment, 105 min, tier 1
  Run at least three commercial or open ASR systems against your set and report per-condition error rates. Then answer the question a buyer in this region would ask: which one would you recommend for a Singapore deployment, and what is the honest caveat? Report the degradation from control to code-switched as a ratio, because that number is the finding.
  *Checkpoints:* three systems benchmarked → per-condition rates reported → degradation ratio and a recommendation
- **M3.6 · Retention: accent, code-switching and evaluation coverage** — retention, 15 min, tier 1

### M5 — Transport and streaming

*390 minutes*

- **M5.1 · WebRTC for the Curious** — reading, 90 min, tier 0  
[WebRTC for the Curious](https://webrtcforthecurious.com/)
  *Where:* The chapters on connecting, media, and jitter and loss.
  Voice agents live or die on transport, and this is the only free text that explains why. Read at least the sections on jitter, packet loss and connection establishment.
- **M5.2 · Understand and improve voice agent latency** — reading, 60 min, tier 0  
[Understand and improve voice agent latency](https://livekit.com/blog/understand-and-improve-agent-latency)
  Vendor-authored, so read the numbers as a floor rather than a promise. The decomposition by hop is the right one.
- **M5.3 · Where the milliseconds actually go** — assignment, 165 min, tier 1
  Build a full loop — microphone to recognition to model to synthesis to speaker — and instrument every hop. Report the budget as a distribution per hop, measured from the user's action. Then state which single hop you would attack first and what it would cost. Most people discover the model is not the problem.
  *Checkpoints:* full loop working → every hop instrumented → per-hop distributions and an attack plan
- **M5.4 · The transport experiment** — assignment, 60 min, tier 2
  Degrade the network deliberately — added latency, jitter, packet loss — and report what your loop does at each level. State the conditions under which it becomes unusable, in terms a customer could check against their own office wifi.
- **M5.5 · Retention: transport, jitter and their latency effects** — retention, 15 min, tier 1

### M6 — Turn-taking and barge-in

*390 minutes*

- **M6.1 · Moshi: a speech-text foundation model for real-time dialogue** — reading, 90 min, tier 0  
[Moshi: a speech-text foundation model for real-time dialogue](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00037)
  Full-duplex: the model listens while it speaks. The clearest argument that turn-taking is architectural rather than a feature you bolt on.
- **M6.2 · HuBERT** — reading, 45 min, tier 0  
[HuBERT: Self-Supervised Speech Representation Learning by Masked Prediction of Hidden Units](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07447)
- **M6.3 · Barge-in that works** — assignment, 165 min, tier 1
  Make your loop interruptible: the user speaks over the agent, the agent stops promptly, and the conversation continues coherently from where it was cut. Measure stop latency from the onset of user speech. Then handle the hard half — distinguishing a genuine interruption from a cough, a background voice, or the user saying 'mm-hm' while agreeing.
  *Checkpoints:* interruption detected and playback stopped → stop latency measured → false-interruption cases handled and reported
- **M6.4 · The awkward pause** — assignment, 75 min, tier 2
  Find the endpointing threshold where your agent starts talking over people, and the one where it feels sluggish. Report both numbers and what you chose. Then say how the right answer differs for a Mandarin speaker, an English speaker, and someone switching between them mid-sentence — because it does, and it is the kind of thing a regional deployment gets wrong.
- **M6.5 · Retention: endpointing, barge-in and duplex behaviour** — retention, 15 min, tier 1

Now attack the same problem with a vision-language model reading the page as an image. *405 minutes*

- **M8.1 · Ship a voice loop** — project, 165 min, tier 3

  Publish this as the next chapter of your public engineering log — one growing site, not a sixth standalone post.     - Your p95 end-to-end is X. Which hop, and what would you have to give up to fix it?
    - Your barge-in triggers on background speech. Talk me through the trade you made and who it hurts.
