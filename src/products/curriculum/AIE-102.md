# AIE-102 — Evaluation and Measurement

**4 credits · 66 hours · 7 modules**

> You have shipped four LLM products and cannot currently answer, with a number, whether any of them is any good. This course fixes that, using those products as the laboratory. Nothing here is a toy dataset.

The highest-value and least-practised skill in applied AI, and the one that makes every later claim in this programme defensible. It is also the course that makes Indie Degree's own grading panel credible — you cannot honestly grade with a judge you have not calibrated.

**By the end you can**

- Build an eval set from production failures rather than from imagination
- Name and measure the known pathologies of LLM judges — position, verbosity, self-enhancement bias
- Calibrate an automated judge against your own labels and report the agreement, not just the score
- Decide whether a difference between two systems is real, with an interval rather than a vibe
- Wire evals into CI so a regression fails a build

**Sitting rule.** Every item is completable in one sitting. Nothing exceeds 180 minutes, and anything over 90 declares its own checkpoints. Pacing is irregular by design, so an item must never require yesterday's working memory to resume.

## The panel

- **Judges** `claude-opus-5`, `gpt-5`, `gemini-flash-latest`
- **Blind** Judges see the submission and the rubric, never each other's scores, and never the learner's self-assessment.
- **Disagreement** Scores are reported per judge and never averaged into a single number when the spread is 2 or more on any criterion. A wide spread is displayed on the transcript as a wide spread — it is information about the assessment's confidence, not noise to be smoothed away.

> **Conflict of interest.** The panel that grades this course is the same mechanism the course teaches the learner to distrust. That is deliberate: assignment A3.2 requires measuring the biases of a judge the learner builds, and A4.2 requires calibrating one against human labels. If the panel here is uncalibrated, the coursework should be able to demonstrate it — and a submission that does so earns credit rather than losing it.

### M2 — Error analysis and the first eval set

*540 minutes*

- **M2.0 · How to Build and Evaluate AI systems in the Age of LLMs** — lecture, 90 min, tier 0

  Collect 50 real generations from 1 Percent More Fluent — actual outputs, not ones you produce for this exercise. Open-code every failure you can see without a predefined taxonomy. Include at least 6 cases where the correct answer is 'no evidence exists' — a set with no negatives measures nothing. Machine-checkable: the harness must run it unattended.
  *Checkpoints:* 24 positive cases → 6+ negative cases → harness runs green

- **M2.7 · What your error analysis missed** — assignment, 30 min, tier 2

### M4 — Calibration against human judgement

*660 minutes*

- **M4.0 · Who Validates the Validators? Aligning LLM-Assisted Evaluation with Human Preferences** — reading, 90 min, tier 0  
[Who Validates the Validators? Aligning LLM-Assisted Evaluation of LLM Outputs with Human Preferences](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12272)
  The criteria you think you want are not the criteria you apply — they drift as you grade. This is the paper the whole module is built on.
- **M4.1 · Chatbot Arena: human preference at scale** — reading, 60 min, tier 0  
[Chatbot Arena: An Open Platform for Evaluating LLMs by Human Preference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04132)
- **M4.2 · Inter-rater agreement** — reading, 45 min, tier 0  
[sklearn.metrics.cohen_kappa_score](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.metrics.cohen_kappa_score.html)
  Understand why raw agreement percentage is misleading and what kappa corrects for.
- **M4.3 · Label fifty cases yourself, blind** — assignment, 120 min, tier 3
  Hand-label 50 cases for the same judgement your M3.7 judge makes. Do this before looking at any judge output — the labels are the reference, and a reference contaminated by the thing it measures is worthless. Record your labelling protocol as you go, including every rule you invent mid-way, because those are where your own inconsistency lives.
  *Checkpoints:* protocol written before labelling → 50 labels done → mid-way rule changes recorded
- **M4.4 · Calibrate to threshold, and show the trajectory** — assignment, 180 min, tier 1
  Compute Cohen's kappa between your judge and your labels. Iterate the judge's rubric until kappa exceeds 0.6, or until you can argue that it cannot be reached and why. Report every iteration's kappa, including the ones that made it worse. The trajectory is the deliverable; the final number alone is not.
  *Checkpoints:* kappa computed for v1 → three iterations logged → threshold reached or argued impossible
- **M4.5 · The disagreement that mattered** — assignment, 90 min, tier 2
  Find the case where your judge and you disagreed most sharply. Argue both sides, then say who was right and why. If it was the judge, say so plainly — that is the more interesting outcome and it is graded no lower.
- **M4.6 · Retention: calibration and agreement statistics** — retention, 15 min, tier 1
- **M4.7 · Re-grade your own advanced standing** — assignment, 60 min, tier 2
  Apply what you now know to the advanced-standing award this programme granted you before you started. Was eval-design at level 2 defensible? Submit a revised assessment with reasoning. Arguing your own credit downward is a valid and fully-credited outcome.

## Completion

- All assignments and the project submitted. Readings are tier 0 and are tracked but never counted toward mastery.
- Deterministic. Each item's tier sets its weight; panel scores enter as per-criterion values; nothing is rounded up. Computed in TypeScript, never by a model.
- Course completion sets initial mastery on the seven eval skills. FSRS scheduling then decays them, so the transcript reports what is currently held rather than what was once passed.
