# AIE-101 — LLM Application Engineering

**3 credits · 48 hours · 7 modules**

> You have already shipped four of these. This course is not here to teach you to call an API — it is here to convert what you did by instinct into something you can specify, defend, and repeat. Two of three credits are already awarded, so most of what follows is exemptable; what is not exemptable is the part that closes your actual gap.

Everything else in Block I assumes you can get reliable, structured, tool-using behaviour out of a model. Four shipped products already evidence most of this.

**By the end you can**

- Specify and enforce structured output such that malformed responses are impossible rather than unlikely
- Design tool interfaces a model can actually use, and explain why a given interface fails
- Name and detect the specific failure modes of a deployed LLM feature
- Manage context deliberately — what goes in, what gets dropped, and what that costs

**Sitting rule.** Every item is completable in one sitting. Nothing exceeds 180 minutes, and anything over 90 declares its own checkpoints.

> **Advanced standing.** Advanced standing awarded 2 of 3 credits against this course. Items marked `advanced_standing_exempt` are already evidenced by shipped work and may be skipped with the artifact cited in place of a submission. The two unexempted assignments — M4.4 and M5.4 — are the whole reason the third credit was withheld: model-directed tool selection, and deliberate measurement of a context-management choice.

  *Where:* All nine chapters and the appendix, in order.
  Work every exercise, including the appendix. Submit your completed notebook with outputs. This is deliberately hands-on rather than a read — the exercises fail visibly when the prompt is wrong, which is the point.
- **M2.7 · Version a prompt like code** — assignment, 60 min, tier 2
  Take one prompt in a shipped product that you have changed at least twice. Reconstruct its history: what each change was meant to fix, and how you decided it worked. Then state what you would need in place to answer that second question with evidence rather than impression. This is the handoff into AIE-102.
- **M2.8 · When the reasoning moved into the model** — reading, 60 min, tier 0  
[Extended thinking](https://docs.claude.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/extended-thinking)
  Everything above this point in the module is from 2022 and 2023, when reasoning was something you coaxed out of a model with a prompt. It is now something the model does. Read this for the part that inverts: with a reasoning model, a high-level instruction to think deeply generally beats the prescriptive step-by-step guidance the earlier material teaches, because the model's own approach can exceed the one you would have prescribed.
- **M2.9 · How reasoning was trained in rather than prompted out** — reading, 75 min, tier 0  
[DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948)
  *Where:* The training method and the emergent-behaviour sections. Read it against the chain-of-thought paper in M2.4: the same behaviour, moved from the prompt into the weights, and the account of what appeared without being asked for.
  The open account of how a reasoning model is made. You do not need the reinforcement learning in detail — AIE-206 does that — but you do need to see that self-checking and backtracking emerged from training rather than from instructions, because that is what makes the old prompting advice obsolete.
- **M2.10 · Prompt a reasoning model the old way, on purpose** — assignment, 120 min, tier 2
  Take a task with a checkable answer. Write two prompts: one in the style M2.1 to M2.5 teaches, with prescribed steps, and one that simply asks the model to think about it thoroughly. Run both on a reasoning model and both on a non-reasoning model — four runs, same task, several repeats each. Record correctness and token cost. The interesting result is the interaction: the prescriptive prompt often helps the older model and hurts the newer one. If that is not what you find, say so and show it, because the claim is common and your measurement outranks it.
  *Checkpoints:* One task chosen where the answer can be judged right or wrong → Prescriptive step-by-step prompt written and run on a reasoning model → High-level 'think deeply' prompt written and run on the same model and task → Both run on a non-reasoning model as the control → Outcomes and token cost recorded for all four
